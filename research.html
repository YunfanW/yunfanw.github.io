My Research

My research examines how diversity in experience, information, and expertise shapes performance and trade-offs in service organizations, with a growing focus on healthcare operations and analytics, including AI-enabled service delivery.

01Job Market PaperSecond-round R&R

Project Team Experiential Diversity, Project Performance, and Voluntary Turnover in the Context of IT and Consulting Services

Yunfan Wang, Fabrizio Salvador, Emmanouil Avgerinos, and Marco Caserta

Second-round revise and resubmit at Production and Operations Management

Should firms keep assigning the same team to a client, or bring in employees with different prior client experience? How does this choice affect project performance and talent retention? We examine these questions using data from a multinational consulting firm.

Research overview +

Project-based firms in knowledge-intensive industries pursue the dual goal of ensuring project profitability and creating a work environment that retains human capital—a key asset in this setting. From an operational standpoint, team composition is a key lever for supporting these two goals, but it presents a theoretical and practical conundrum. On the one hand, assigning employees to experientially diverse teams creates learning opportunities, which should improve performance. On the other hand, as learning increases employees’ market value, it can paradoxically also raise the risk of voluntary turnover. It can potentially harm performance as well, because creating experientially diverse teams implies giving up the potential benefits of stable, specialized teams. In this study, we investigate the effect of team experiential diversity using data from 810 project teams, involving 3,139 employees drawn from a multinational consulting and IT services firm. We focus on team experiential diversity relative to customer experience (i.e., client experience diversity) and familiarity among team members (i.e., coworker experience diversity) and find that both facets of team experiential diversity increase project performance but have an inverted U-shaped relationship with voluntary turnover. Overall, our results indicate that, below a critical threshold, changes in team experiential diversity entail a performance-retention trade-off that must be considered when composing project teams, and that is different from other trade-offs identified by research in voluntary work settings.

02Working PaperComplete draft

Talk More or Talk More Diverse? Topic Variety, Content Distribution, and Sentiment in Online Hotel Reviews

Yunfan Wang, Emmanouil Avgerinos, Konstantina Valogianni, and Berend Dumas

Complete draft; planned submission to Information Systems Research

Does the way customers allocate attention across topics affect the sentiment they express in online reviews?

Research overview +

The study examines information diversity in customer-generated review text. We conceptualize review diversity along two dimensions: topic variety, which captures the breadth of service attributes discussed in a review, and content distribution, which captures the extent to which review content is concentrated on a subset of those attributes. Using 119,630 English Booking.com reviews from 110 hotels in Amsterdam, Brussels, and London, we build an LLM-based pipeline to identify service topics and classify review sentiment. The results show that topic variety is positively associated with review sentiment, whereas content distribution is negatively associated with review sentiment; both effects are stronger among business travelers. The main contribution is to show that review sentiment is shaped not only by the service experience but also by how customers organize that experience in their writing. The paper also has practical implications for platform design and post-stay review solicitation, where prompts can encourage more informative and balanced customer feedback.

03Working PaperComplete draft

The Effect of Non-Clinical Workforce on Service Efficiency: Evidence from the NHS Medical Helpline

Yunfan Wang, Emmanouil Avgerinos, Georgios Batsakis, Nikolaos Karouzakis, and Yufei Huang

Complete draft; planned submission to Human Resource Management

What can earlier forms of algorithm-supported work teach us about AI and workforce substitution? We study NHS 111, where non-clinical call handlers use algorithms to take on tasks traditionally performed by clinical advisors, and examine the consequences for service efficiency and patient outcomes.

Research overview +

The study focuses on NHS 111 medical helpline call centers, where non-clinical call handlers and clinical advisors jointly provide advice for urgent but non-life-threatening medical issues. Using 1,105 provider-month observations from 32 NHS 111 call centers, we examine how the non-clinical labor mix—the ratio of time worked by call handlers to time worked by clinical advisors—affects service efficiency and patient-facing outcomes. We find that a higher non-clinical labor mix improves efficiency by increasing timely call-backs, but also increases referrals to other healthcare services, reduces self-care recommendations, and is associated with lower patient compliance. These results show that shifting work to non-clinical staff can increase speed and access, but it may also push costs to other parts of the healthcare system. The paper contributes to healthcare operations and workforce-management research by showing that labor substitution should be evaluated not only by front-end efficiency, but also by downstream service use and patient behavior.
